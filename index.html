PROJECT OVERVIEW

SPACESUIT THERMAL SHIELDING KIT

This experiment evaluates the transient cooling behaviour of a water-filled test capsule with different insulation configurations. Measured temperature histories are compared with a first-order Newton cooling model.

The project provides a simplified study of thermal resistance, experimental uncertainty and model validation. It is inspired by multilayer thermal-control systems, but the ice-water bath is a terrestrial conductive and convective environment rather than a simulation of vacuum.

The warm water represents the protected thermal mass, while the ice-water bath provides a repeatable low-temperature boundary condition.

Before testing, calculate a reference cooling curve. During the experiment, record temperatures at fixed intervals and quantify model agreement using residuals and mean absolute error (MAE).

Learning outcomes: Apply lumped-parameter heat-transfer modelling, construct a controlled test article, acquire temperature data and evaluate model limitations.

UNDERSTANDING HEAT TRANSFER: THREE PHYSICAL MODES CORE SCIENCE INFOGRAPHIC

Heat always naturally moves from a warmer object to a colder object through three distinct physical mechanisms. Understanding these processes is essential to designing effective spacesuit thermal protection:

1. CONDUCTION

Heat passing through direct contact as vibrating particles bump into neighboring molecules.

2. CONVECTION

Heat carried by circulating currents of fluids (liquids/gases) rising when warm and sinking when cold.

3. RADIATION

Heat emitted as electromagnetic infrared light waves capable of traveling through a total vacuum.

HEAT-TRANSFER MECHANISMS IN THE TEST ASSEMBLY

COTTON SHEETS — REDUCING CONDUCTION

Mechanism: Cotton contains many small air pockets. Because air is a poor conductor of heat, cotton slows the transfer of thermal energy from the warm container to the colder surroundings.

BUBBLE WRAP — REDUCING CONVECTION

Mechanism: Bubble wrap traps air inside sealed pockets. Because the air cannot move freely, convection currents are reduced and heat is retained more effectively.

MYLAR FOIL — REDUCING RADIATION

Mechanism: Reflective film has low emissivity and can reduce radiative heat transfer when separated by an air gap. Flight multilayer insulation uses specialised reflective layers and spacers under vacuum; the classroom assembly is not equivalent to flight hardware.

NEWTON'S LAW OF COOLING
What fundamental equation governs the rate of heat loss of a hot core capsule submerged in a cooler ambient fluid?
[ TAP TO REVEAL FORMULA ]
EXPONENTIAL DECAY
$$T(t) = T_{\text{env}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{env}})e^{-kt}$$
The cooling rate $\frac{dT}{dt}$ is directly proportional to the instantaneous temperature gap $(T(t) - T_{\text{env}})$.
DECAY CONSTANT (k)
What does the constant $k$ quantify physically, and which capsule is better insulated: $k = 0.15\text{ min}^{-1}$ or $k = 0.015\text{ min}^{-1}$?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
LOWER-k CAPSULE ($k = 0.015\text{ min}^{-1}$)
For a lumped system, $k = \frac{hA}{mc}$ is an effective cooling constant. Under comparable conditions, a smaller $k$ produces a slower temperature decay.
FOURIER'S LAW OF CONDUCTION
How does thermal energy transfer directly through solid matter, and what formula defines conduction heat flow?
[ TAP TO REVEAL FORMULA ]
MOLECULAR CONDUCTION
$$\dot Q = -\lambda A \frac{dT}{dx}$$
Heat conducts from warm to cold via direct microscopic particle collisions, governed by thermal conductivity $\lambda$ and area $A$.
THERMAL EQUILIBRIUM
When does net heat transfer between the capsule core and the $0^\circ\text{C}$ ice bath drop to zero? What physical law governs this?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
ZEROTH LAW EQUILIBRIUM
$$T_{\text{capsule}} = T_{\text{env}}$$
When capsule temperature equals environment temperature, net heat flow stops completely ($\frac{dQ}{dt} = 0$).
SPACE VACUUM RADIATION
Which heat-transfer mechanism can cross a vacuum between separated bodies, and what law governs it?
[ TAP TO REVEAL FORMULA ]
STEFAN-BOLTZMANN RADIATION
$$P_{\text{rad}} = \epsilon \sigma A (T^4 - T_{\text{env}}^4)$$
Thermal radiation crosses a vacuum as electromagnetic photons. Conduction cannot occur across empty space without direct physical contact.
MYLAR RADIATIVE SHIELD
How does thin shiny aluminized Mylar foil protect spacecraft and astronauts from extreme solar thermal radiation?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
INFRARED REFLECTANCE
An aluminized reflective film has high infrared reflectance and low emissivity ($\epsilon$), greatly reducing radiative heat transfer.
CONVECTIVE AIR TRAPPING
How do the sealed air cells in bubble wrap reduce thermal energy transfer?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
STAGNANT AIR POCKETS
Trapped air has low thermal conductivity ($\lambda_{\text{air}} \approx 0.026\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$), while small sealed cells suppress bulk fluid convection circulation.
MULTI-LAYER INSULATION (MLI)
Why do spacesuits and satellites stack Cotton + Bubble Wrap + Mylar in composite layers?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
TRIPLE HEAT SHIELD
Composite synergy: Cotton blocks solid conduction, Bubble Wrap suppresses fluid convection, and Mylar reflects infrared radiation.
ANALYTICAL REFERENCE MODEL
What key assumptions does the kit's Newton cooling model make?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
LUMPED-PARAMETER MODEL
It assumes a uniform capsule core temperature, a constant bath temperature, and a constant effective cooling coefficient $k$.
ICE-WATER BOUNDARY
Why is an ice-water slurry used in the comparative experiment?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
CONSTANT HEAT SINK
An ice-water slurry provides a stable, repeatable cold boundary ($0.0^\circ\text{C}$). Measure its actual temperature for accurate model comparisons.
HEAT CAPACITY OF WATER
Why is liquid water ($c = 4184\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$) ideal for capsule core telemetry trials? What is the heat formula?
[ TAP TO REVEAL FORMULA ]
HIGH THERMAL MASS
$$Q = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T$$
Water stores large thermal energy per gram, producing smooth, measurable exponential decay over $15+\text{ minutes}$ without dropping instantly.
DECAY CURVE CURVATURE
Why is the temperature-time graph $T(t)$ steep at $t = 0$ and increasingly flat toward $t = 15\text{ min}$?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
SHRINKING TEMP GAP
Cooling rate is proportional to $(T(t) - T_{\text{env}})$. As temperature difference decreases, cooling rate slows down asymptotically!
MEAN ABSOLUTE ERROR (MAE)
How is the Mean Absolute Error between measured telemetry and predicted temperatures calculated?
[ TAP TO REVEAL FORMULA ]
MODEL RESIDUALS
$$\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|T_{\mathrm{meas},i} - T_{\mathrm{model},i}|$$
MAE quantifies the average magnitude of temperature prediction errors across all measured time steps in $^\circ\text{C}$.
PROBE PLACEMENT PROTOCOL
Why should the thermometer probe be positioned near the centre of the liquid rather than touching the capsule wall?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
BULK FLUID TEMPERATURE
Wall contact biases readings toward local boundary layer temperatures rather than the bulk core liquid temperature, introducing systematic error.
SI UNITS REFERENCE
What are the standard SI units for temperature $T$, thermal energy $Q$, thermal conductivity $\lambda$, and cooling constant $k$?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
UNITS CHEAT SHEET
• Temperature ($T$): $\text{K}$ or $^\circ\text{C}$
• Energy ($Q$): Joules ($\text{J}$)
• Decay Constant ($k$): $\text{min}^{-1}$ or $\text{s}^{-1}$
• Conductivity ($\lambda$): $\text{W}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{K})$
MULTILAYER CONFIGURATION
Which tested configuration achieves the smallest cooling constant $k$, and why?
[ TAP TO REVEAL ANSWER ]
OPTIMAL THERMAL RESISTANCE
The combined Cotton + Bubble Wrap + Reflective Mylar assembly provides maximum resistance ($k \approx 0.015\text{ min}^{-1}$) by tackling all 3 heat transfer modes.

INTERACTIVE PRACTICE: HOW TO CALCULATE YOUR DECAY CONSTANT (k)

STEP-BY-STEP MATH TOOL

The decay constant $k$ measures how fast heat leaks out of your capsule. You can calculate your capsule's exact $k$-value from your lab thermometer readings using this formula derived from Newton's Law of Cooling:

$$k = -\frac{\ln\left(\frac{T(t) - T_{\text{env}}}{T_0 - T_{\text{env}}}\right)}{t}$$
CALCULATED RESULTS & STEP-BY-STEP BREAKDOWN:
$$\text{Temp Ratio} = \frac{43.9 - 0.0}{80.0 - 0.0} = 0.5488$$
$$\ln(0.5488) = -0.6003$$
$$k = -\frac{-0.6003}{15} = 0.040\text{ min}^{-1}$$
CLOSEST ASSUMED REFERENCE: BUBBLE-WRAP TEST ASSEMBLY ($k \approx 0.040\text{ min}^{-1}$)

PRACTICE PROBLEMS FOR CLASSROOM REINFORCEMENT

PRACTICE PROBLEM #01
An astronaut capsule is filled with $T_0 = 80^\circ\text{C}$ hot water and placed into a $T_{\text{env}} = 0^\circ\text{C}$ ice bath. After $t = 15\text{ min}$, its thermometer reads $T(15) = 63.8^\circ\text{C}$. What is the capsule's insulation decay constant $k$?
💡 Hint: Extract values: $T_0 = 80^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{\text{env}} = 0^\circ\text{C}$, $T(15) = 63.8^\circ\text{C}$, $t = 15\text{ min}$. Plug into:
$$k = -\frac{\ln\left(\frac{63.8 - 0}{80 - 0}\right)}{15}$$
✓ Solution:
$$\text{Temp Ratio} = \frac{63.8}{80} = 0.7975 \implies \ln(0.7975) = -0.2263$$
$$k = -\frac{-0.2263}{15} = 0.015\text{ min}^{-1}$$
This matches the assumed cooling constant for the multilayer test configuration.
PRACTICE PROBLEM #02
If a Mylar-wrapped capsule has $k = 0.121\text{ min}^{-1}$ and starts at $T_0 = 80^\circ\text{C}$ in a $T_{\text{env}} = 0^\circ\text{C}$ ice bath, what will its temperature be after $t = 20\text{ minutes}$?
💡 Hint: Use Newton's cooling formula:
$$T(20) = 0 + (80 - 0) \cdot e^{-0.121 \times 20}$$
✓ Solution:
$$e^{-0.121 \times 20} = e^{-2.42} = 0.0889$$
$$T(20) = 80 \times 0.0889 = 7.1^\circ\text{C}$$
